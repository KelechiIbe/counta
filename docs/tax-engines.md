# Tax engines: companies income tax, VAT and withholding

Accounting, Tax returns has three tabs. Each reads the same books the statements are drawn from, so a figure on a return can always be traced to journals.

## Companies income tax

**Years and law.** A company's years run from 2023 accounts (or the first year of its books, if later) to the year running now. The year of assessment is the accounting year plus one. Accounts for 2023 and 2024 use the old law (`supabase/rulebooks/ng-cit-cita-legacy.json`: CIT by size at 0/20/30%, tertiary education tax, minimum tax, Second Schedule capital allowances with initial and annual allowances, the two-thirds restriction and ₦10 retention). Accounts for 2025 onwards use the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 (`ng-cit-nta-2025.json`: small companies exempt, 30% otherwise, 4% development levy, 15% minimum effective tax, interest limited to 30% of EBITDA for connected persons, First Schedule allowances on cost with 1% retained). `private.rulebook(yoa)` picks the rule book; both live in `cit_rulebooks`.

**Where the figures come from.** A year is computed from the books when they cover it, and otherwise from figures read from the audited accounts or a trial balance (`cit_years.source = imported`). The AI reader (`ai` function, action `cit_map`) places each figure on the most specific input line of the Rev360 template (`cit_template_lines`, 260 lines); several source lines on one template line add up and keep the lowest confidence. Anything can be corrected by hand: a books line is overridden with a reason, an imported line is replaced and marked as entered.

**The computation.** `private.cit_compute(org, year, bf)` starts from profit before tax as the template computes it (INCOME_STATEMENT!E115), adds back disallowable expenses (PROFIT_ADJUSTMENT rows 14 to 32: depreciation and donations above the limit from the books by the rule book, the rest as items with reasons), deducts non-taxable income (rows 36 to 40), then applies loss relief, capital allowances, the tax rates and the levies. `private.cit_chain` runs the years in order so losses, unused allowances and disallowed interest carry forward; an approved year carries forward what it was approved with. The first year Counta prepares takes its brought-forward amounts from the year's settings; a later year can use different amounts only with a reason.

**Capital allowances.** Every asset in the fixed asset register, and assets kept for tax only (`tax_assets`, for things bought before the books), has a Rev360 category that decides its class and rate. An asset's tax value is rolled forward from the year it was first used, under the law of each year, unless an opening tax value from the last return filed is given.

**Checks.** A year cannot be approved while anything blocks: the audited accounts are not attached, the balance sheet does not balance, the template's profit or revenue differs from the audited accounts read in, the year has not ended, the business is not a company or is under a regime Counta does not compute. Warnings (lines on "Others", categories missing, rules awaiting sign-off) do not block.

**Approval and filing.** Approving (owner, or someone who approves adjustments) freezes the computation, the template cells, the fixed asset declaration and the company details. The `tax-files` function fills the Rev360 template (`public/tax-templates/rev360-cit-v2.xlsx`, pinned by SHA-256) keeping its formulas and recalculating their cached values, checks five ties against the computation, and stores the approved file once. Sending to Flux is described in `flux-cit-handoff.md`. The financial statements' tax computation and `post_tax_charge` read the same engine.

## VAT

**Rates on items.** Each item is standard-rated (7.5%), zero-rated or exempt. The AI classifier works from the rule book's lists (zero-rated (a) to (s), exempt (a) to (n)); domestic petroleum products, sugar, sparkling and restaurant water, solar equipment and ride-hailing are standard-rated. An invoice with exempt lines says "This supply is exempted from VAT", and one that is wholly exempt has no VAT line.

**Input VAT.** Bills from VAT-registered suppliers carry 7.5% on standard-rated lines; the business claims all of it (lines can be marked not recoverable). Services bought from a supplier abroad that does not charge Nigerian VAT have 7.5% self-charged when the bill is approved (Dr 1402, Cr 2211) and paid to the NRS by the 14th. A business that is a withholding VAT agent keeps back the VAT on its suppliers' bills when paying them (Cr 2211) and pays it over by the 14th. VAT that a customer who is a withholding VAT agent kept is recorded with the payment (Dr 1405) and credited in the return.

**Imports and stock written off.** A customs entry (`save_customs_entry`) records goods imported: Form M, bill of lading, vessel, country, HS code, quantity, the invoice value and the customs exchange rate, the duty and charges in the VAT base, and the import VAT paid. The VAT is input VAT (Dr 1402) paid from a money account, or already in the books from a bank line (the entry then links that journal and posts nothing). Duty and clearing charges go on the stock receipt as landed costs. Standard-rated stock written off (posted to the stock loss account: breakage, expiry, theft, quarantined batches, other losses; not miscounts or unrecorded sales) has its input VAT taken back at 7.5% of cost in the month it is written off; filing the month moves that VAT from 1402 to the stock loss account.

**The month.** `private.vat_month` adds up output VAT on sales (less credit notes), input VAT on bills, stock receipts and customs entries (with the self-charged VAT, less suppliers' credit notes and the VAT on stock written off) and VAT withheld by customers. `private.vat_chain` runs month by month from the start of the books: net VAT = output − withheld by customers − input. A positive net is paid after using credit brought forward (oldest first); a negative net becomes credit carried forward, kept in layers by the month it arose. Credit older than five years lapses (to 6512); a refund can be claimed on credit from the last 12 months. Filing a month freezes its figures and clears 2201, 1402 and 1405 into what is payable; paying it clears 2201. A filed month can be reopened until it is paid.

**The NRS return.** `vat_schedule(org, month)` lays the month out on the NRS VAT return (Form VAT 002, `public/tax-templates/nrs-vat-v1.xlsx`, pinned by SHA-256) and `tax-files` (action `vat_file`) fills the workbook, leaving the form's lines to the template's own formulas and checking each against Counta:
- A, sales: every sale line by customer, product, VAT status (0, 1, 2), the NRS description of exempt and zero-rated supplies (the template's own list, plus the Act's words for baby products and for assistive devices and disability-related products, which the list lacks: chosen on the item or the line, else suggested by the tax agent or from the item's or line's words, and applied without asking; exports are exported goods or exported services) and the place of consumption: the customer's state and LGA (its state from its address when not chosen), else the branch's. Walk-in and till sales are one row per product per branch, named "Walk-in customers". Credit notes on exempt and zero-rated lines are netted against the product they credit.
- B, sales adjustments: one row per credit note on standard-rated sales, against the invoice it credits; the table grows past its 29 rows.
- C, purchases: standard-rated purchases whose VAT is claimed, by bill and stock receipt, as goods (stock, cost of sales, supplies of goods), services, or fixed assets (asset accounts); suppliers' credit notes are the reconciliation column; the zero-rated part is apportioned by the month's zero-rated share of VATable sales. Opening and closing stock are left blank; C1 lists stock written off.
- D, imports: the customs entries. E, self-charged VAT: services (and goods) from suppliers abroad, in the business's state. F: VAT kept by withholding agents, against the invoice's own value before VAT (its VATable lines less credit notes on them, shared across the agent's payments on it by the VAT each kept; a payment with no invoice takes the value its VAT implies).
- Lines 116 to 150 (credit brought forward, credit, payable) are left to the portal. Line 115 ties to Counta's net VAT for the month within a kobo a row (the template works VAT out on totals). Where an agent kept a different amount from 7.5% of the invoice value, line 110 credits the 7.5% and the tie leaves that gap out (`wvat_gap`). The return lists what to fix first: a branch or customer without its state or LGA, an exempt or zero-rated sale whose words fit no NRS description, an agent that kept a different amount (`wvat_partial`), and a notice when a description off the template's list is used (`label_off_list`).

**Whether to charge VAT.** `vat_status` judges the small company test on the last full financial year (turnover up to ₦100 million, fixed assets up to ₦250 million, no professional services). Every morning `vat_threshold_tick` tells the owner of a business not charging VAT once sales this year pass ₦100 million. Only the owner turns VAT on or off.

## Withholding tax

**Rates.** `wht_rates` holds the NRS table: a rate for each transaction type for a company or not, in Nigeria or abroad (null where the transaction does not apply), the NRS transaction code, and the exemptions (a) to (m). The rate doubles, without a cap, when the supplier has no Tax ID, as the NRS template computes it. Nothing is withheld from a government body, an exempt supplier, or a manufacturer on its own goods. A small company need not withhold from a supplier with a Tax ID while the month's bills from that supplier stay within ₦2 million. Compensation for loss of office is 5% of the amount above ₦50 million.

**Bills.** Each bill line can have its own transaction type; the rest follow the bill, which follows the supplier. `private.bill_wht` works out each line and notes why a line has none. Payments keep back WHT (and withholding VAT) in proportion to what they settle and the rest with the last payment, by the wallet, the bank, bank matching, the books inbox and bulk payments alike.

**Where it goes.** WHT from a company or a supplier abroad goes to the NRS by the 21st of the next month; from anyone else (business names, sole traders, partnerships, individuals) to the state revenue service of the supplier's state by the 30th (the last day of February). No state is assumed (sign-off W-2): until the supplier's state is added, a bill whose WHT goes to a state cannot be approved or paid from Counta, and a payment recorded from the bank leaves its deduction waiting with no authority (`state_missing`), which blocks that month's state schedule and moves to the right state, with its due date, once the state is saved. Withholding VAT goes to the NRS by the 14th.

**Sending a bill back.** A draft or approved bill that has not been paid can go back to the supplier (`return_bill`): for a missing Tax ID (sign-off W-1; the note points to taxid.nrs.gov.ng, NIN for an individual, CAC number for a business), for a missing address (W-2), or for another reason in the user's own words. It leaves the books as a void bill does, keeps the reason and the note, and the note goes to the supplier by email or WhatsApp (template `counta_bill_returned`) when asked. A Prism bill shows the reason, the note and the date on its `/prism/bill-status`. `reopen_bill` makes it a draft again when the supplier answers. A user may instead keep a bill from a supplier with no Tax ID and withhold at double the rate. The ₦2 million small-company limit is measured on the month's bills; bills approved earlier in a month that later passes the limit are left as they are.

**Records.** Each paid deduction becomes a row in `wht_deductions` with a receipt number (`WHT-00001`), the supplier's ID (Tax ID, else RC number, else NIN) and the month. `wht_schedule` lists a month for one authority in its template's columns; `tax-files` fills the NRS template (130 lines a file) or the state template. `record_wht_remittance` pays a month over (Dr 2202 or 2211, Cr the bank) and marks its deductions remitted. Suppliers get receipts with payer, payee, nature, amount, rate and month. A supplier's NIN is kept apart from the supplier record, in `vendor_nins`, which no signed-in role reads directly: it reaches the screen only through `vendor_tax_details` (owners, people who handle money and people who approve purchases), on the bill and on the supplier's WHT receipt.

## Signed off by TaxStreem, and what is left open

TaxStreem (Kelechi Ibe) signed off the rules on 18 October 2026: the old-law rule book's flags L-A to L-G, T-1 (Rev360 takes the same template for prior years), V-1 to V-4 (the five-year limit on VAT credit runs from the month the credit arose), W-3 (the ₦2 million small-company limit is measured on the month's bills), W-4 and W-5. W-1 and W-2 were changed: a bill from a supplier with no Tax ID can be sent back (or kept, withholding at double the rate), and no supplier state is assumed. The NRS VAT return follows TaxStreem's answers of the same day: input VAT on purchases less stock written off, opening and closing stock left blank, the customer's place else the branch's, walk-in sales by product and branch, the zero-rated part of purchases by the month's zero-rated share of sales.

TaxStreem answered the six VAT return points on 20 October 2026 (migration `20261029000200_vat_return_points.sql`):
- N-1, agreed: the zero-rated share is taken of VATable sales (standard-rated plus zero-rated), since schedule C lists purchases relating to VATable sales.
- N-2, agreed: VAT is taken back on standard-rated stock written off at 7.5% of its cost, whether or not that stock was bought from a supplier who charged VAT.
- N-3, left to Counta: baby products (s.186(1)(d)) and assistive devices and disability-related products (s.186(1)(o)) take the Act's words as their description (`nrs_vat_labels.on_template` is false for them), and Counta applies the description an item's words point to without asking. Live cattle, goats, sheep and poultry stay "Basic food items". If the portal refuses a description off its list, the user picks the nearest one on it.
- N-4, agreed: VAT self-charged on services from abroad goes on schedule E as "Self Charge", under the template's "VATable Local Services Received" category, placed in the business's state.
- N-5, changed: schedule F takes the actual invoice value, and a check flags an agent that kept a different amount from 7.5% of it.
- N-6, agreed: customs entries are schedule D rows with duty and charges as "other charges", and the file is named year, Tax ID, VAT, month (as the NRS sample `2026_2622433961280_VAT_1.xlsx` appears to be).
